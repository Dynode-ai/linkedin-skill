# Goal playbooks

The right profile and content depend on what the user wants LinkedIn to do for them. Identify the primary goal (one, and a secondary at most) before advising. Most bad LinkedIn advice is good advice for a different goal: a job seeker writing like a creator, or a founder writing a résumé.

Each playbook covers: **who is judging the profile**, **profile priorities**, **settings**, **content**, **engagement**, and **what to measure**.

## 1. Get hired (active job search)

**Who judges:** recruiters searching in LinkedIn Recruiter (keyword and filter driven, skimming dozens of profiles fast) and hiring managers who click through from an application.

**Profile priorities**
- **Headline = the target role, not the current one** (if different), plus 2-3 high-value skills and one proof point. Recruiters search by title and skills, so they must appear verbatim: "Machine Learning Engineer" matches, "ML wizard" does not.
- **Current/most recent title** uses the standard market title (add the internal title in the description if it's unusual).
- **Skills section** mirrors the skills in 3-5 target job descriptions. Pin the most relevant ones as top skills.
- **Experience** bullets show outcomes with numbers, using the same keywords as target JDs. The last 2 roles matter most.
- **About** opens with what role they're targeting and why they're good at it, then proof, then what they're looking for.
- **Featured** shows work samples: portfolio, GitHub, case study, talk, article.
- **Location** is set to where they want to work (and open to remote if true). Recruiters filter by location.

**Settings**
- Open to Work: "Recruiters only" if currently employed (it's not shown publicly), public #OpenToWork frame if unemployed and comfortable with it. Fill in titles, locations, start date and work types precisely; recruiters filter on these.
- Primary button: Connect (job seekers benefit from connections with recruiters and peers).

**Content:** optional but powerful. Short posts showing the work: a project breakdown, a lesson from a hard problem, a teardown in their field. 1-2 per week beats nothing. Avoid "I'm looking for a job, please help" as the only post. One clear "what I'm looking for" post is fine, and works best when it lists target roles, strengths and proof.

**Engagement:** thoughtful comments on posts by people at target companies and by hiring managers in their field. Follow target company pages.

**Measure:** search appearances (weekly), recruiter messages, profile views from target companies, interviews booked.

**Use a JD match.** If the user provides target job descriptions, the audit should extract keywords and show gaps (`audit.md` explains how).

## 2. Career growth (employed, building reputation)

**Who judges:** peers, leaders in their company and industry, future employers who aren't hiring yet, conference organizers.

**Profile priorities:** headline states expertise and the problems they solve, not just their title. About tells their professional story and point of view. Featured holds their best talks, articles and posts.

**Settings:** Follow as primary button only if they plan to post regularly and want followers; otherwise Connect.

**Content:** "learning in public" and expertise. Lessons from their work (within confidentiality), frameworks they use, opinions on industry shifts. Check the employer's social media policy and keep confidential details out.

**Measure:** followers in their field, inbound opportunities (speaking, recruiters, collaborations), comments from respected peers.

## 3. Founder / startup

**Who judges:** potential customers (the ICP), investors, potential hires, partners.

**Profile priorities**
- **Headline:** who the company helps and what result, plus founder role. "Co-founder @ Acme" alone tells a buyer nothing. Better: "Helping dental clinics cut no-shows by 30% | Co-founder & CEO @ Acme".
- **About:** the problem they're obsessed with, why them, proof (customers, results, traction they're happy to share), and a clear CTA (book a demo, DM, newsletter).
- **Featured:** product demo, best customer story, a lead magnet or newsletter, a press mention.
- **Experience:** the current company entry sells the mission; earlier roles explain the founder-market fit.

**Settings:** Follow as primary button once posting regularly. Custom button or website link to a demo or landing page if available. Company page linked.

**Content:** the founder's personal account usually reaches far more people than the company page. Mix: build-in-public (decisions, numbers they're comfortable sharing, lessons), customer problems and stories, contrarian industry takes, hiring and culture, product only occasionally and always through a story or problem.

**Engagement:** comment where the ICP hangs out. Founder peers are nice, but buyers pay the bills.

**Measure:** inbound DMs and demo requests, profile views from ICP job titles, follower growth within the ICP (analytics demographics), opportunities sourced from LinkedIn.

## 4. Freelancer, consultant, agency

**Who judges:** potential clients deciding "can this person solve my problem, and is it safe to hire them?"

**Profile priorities**
- **Headline:** specific offer for a specific client: "Webflow sites for B2B SaaS that convert demo traffic | 40+ launches". Specialists win over generalists.
- **About:** client problem → how they solve it → proof (results, named clients if allowed, testimonials) → how to work together → CTA.
- **Services page** filled in (it makes them findable in service searches and can collect reviews).
- **Featured:** case studies with numbers, portfolio, booking link, testimonials.
- **Recommendations:** recent and specific ones from clients are worth more here than for any other goal.

**Settings:** Follow as primary (if posting) or Connect; booking link in the contact/website section and Featured.

**Content:** proof-heavy. Client stories (with permission), before/after, teardowns of common problems, frameworks, mistakes clients make, pricing and process transparency. Every few posts, a soft offer ("I have 2 slots in March for...").

**Measure:** inbound leads, discovery calls booked, profile views from ICP titles.

## 5. Creator / audience builder

**Who judges:** potential followers deciding "will this person's posts be worth seeing in my feed?" and later sponsors or buyers of their products.

**Profile priorities:** headline promises the value of following ("Weekly breakdowns of how B2B companies grow"). About explains what they post and why to follow, with proof (audience size, newsletter subscribers, notable work). Featured: newsletter, best posts, lead magnet, product.

**Settings:** Follow as primary button. LinkedIn newsletter if they can commit to a cadence.

**Content:** consistency and a recognizable format matter most. 3-5 posts per week across 3-4 pillars, recurring series, carousels and native video when it fits. Watch saves and comments more than likes.

**Engagement:** comment daily on posts by creators with overlapping audiences; collaborate (co-posts, guest posts in newsletters).

**Measure:** follower growth, engagement rate, saves, newsletter subscribers, profile visits per post.

## 6. Sales and business development

**Who judges:** prospects who look the seller up before or after an outreach message.

**Profile priorities:** buyer-centric, not quota-centric. "President's Club 3x" impresses sales managers, not buyers. Headline: who they help and what problem they solve. About: how they help customers, customer outcomes, industry insight, how to reach them. Featured: customer stories, useful resources.

**Content:** insight for buyers (industry trends, common mistakes, benchmarks), customer stories, their own POV. Posting even weekly makes cold outreach warmer because prospects see a real person.

**Engagement:** comment on prospects' and customers' posts (genuinely, without pitching). Personalized connection requests referencing something specific.

**Measure:** reply rate on outreach, meetings sourced, profile views from target accounts.

## 7. Executive / senior leader

**Who judges:** employees and candidates, customers, investors, board members, press.

**Profile priorities:** credibility and narrative. Headline: role and mandate ("CFO @ Acme | Scaling finance teams through hypergrowth"). About: leadership philosophy, track record, what the company is building. Featured: interviews, talks, company milestones.

**Content:** company narrative, leadership lessons, industry perspective, celebrating the team (specifically, not generically). Ghostwritten content must still sound like them, so build a voice profile from interview transcripts.

**Measure:** reach among employees, candidates and customers; inbound press and speaking invitations; candidate mentions in interviews.

## 8. Student / new graduate

**Who judges:** recruiters for internships and entry-level roles, alumni, professors.

**Profile priorities:** headline states the target role and current studies ("Computer Science @ UT Austin '27 | Seeking SWE internships | Python, React"). Projects section and Featured do the work experience can't yet. Education with relevant coursework, clubs and awards. Skills matching target roles.

**Settings:** Open to Work with internship or entry-level types. Connect as primary.

**Content:** learning in public, project write-ups, lessons from internships and competitions. Comment on alumni and recruiter posts.

**Measure:** recruiter messages, alumni replies, interviews.

## 9. Career changer

**Who judges:** recruiters and hiring managers in the new field who will ask "why should we bet on someone without the standard background?"

**Profile priorities:** headline leads with the target identity plus the bridge ("Product Designer | Former ER nurse designing healthcare tools clinicians actually use"). About tells the bridge story: what transfers, what they've done to build new skills (courses, projects, freelance work), and evidence they can do the new job. Featured holds projects in the new field. Rewrite past experience bullets around transferable skills, using the new field's vocabulary.

**Content:** documenting the transition: projects, lessons, how their old field gives them a unique angle in the new one. That unique angle is the biggest asset.

**Measure:** conversations with people in the target field, interviews, project feedback.

## 10. Mixed goals

Many users have two goals (for example a consultant who's also open to full-time roles, or a founder who's also hiring). Choose one primary goal for the headline and About. Serve the secondary goal with Featured items, Open to Work set to recruiters only, or a line in the About. A profile that tries to speak to everyone speaks to no one.
