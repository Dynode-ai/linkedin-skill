## 1. Strategy: topics, pillars, funnel

**Topic architecture.** One core topic the user wants to be known for, plus 2-4 subtopics. Roughly 80% of posts should sit inside this territory. LinkedIn's systems match posts and profiles by topic, and audiences follow people for a predictable kind of value. The core topic should appear in the headline and About.

**Pillars** are the recurring categories that fill the calendar. Each pillar is a *topic* with a *purpose*:

| Purpose | What it does | Typical share |
|---|---|---|
| **Teach** (expertise) | Frameworks, how-tos, mistakes, breakdowns. Earns saves and authority. | 30-40% |
| **Show** (proof) | Results, case studies, client stories, work samples, build-in-public. Earns trust. | 20-30% |
| **Think** (perspective) | Opinions, predictions, takes on industry news, contrarian views from experience. Earns comments and recall. | 15-25% |
| **Be human** (story) | Career moments, failures, behind the scenes, values. Earns connection. Story hooks have the best engagement in large datasets. | 10-20% |
| **Offer** (convert) | What they sell or want (hiring, services, product, newsletter, job search). Promotional posts perform fine when useful. | 5-15% |

These shares are heuristics, not data-backed ratios. Adjust them to the goal: job seekers lean into Teach and Show, founders into Show and Think, creators into Teach and Be human.

**Example (fractional CFO for SaaS startups):** (1) *Startup finance mistakes*: Teach. (2) *Fundraising and due diligence from the inside*: Show + Think. (3) *Metrics that matter* (SaaS benchmarks, unit economics): Teach. (4) *The fractional life* (behind the scenes of running a solo practice): Be human + Offer.

**Funnel.** Posts create discovery (reach), trust (proof and consistency) and conversion (clear next step). A healthy month has all three. Conversion happens on the profile, the Featured section, DMs and the newsletter, so the profile must be ready before a content push.

## 2. The idea engine (sources × angles)

Great ideas combine **raw material only the user has** with **an angle that makes it useful**.

**Sources of raw material** (check these in order):
1. **Story bank** (`stories.md`): wins, failures, client stories, career moments.
2. **Their work this week**: decisions made, problems solved, questions from clients or colleagues, tools used.
3. **Audience questions and pains**: what the target audience asks, complains about, or misunderstands (from the brief, niche research, comments, DMs).
4. **Their data**: what has performed for them (`learnings.md`), numbers from their work they can share.
5. **Opinions they've earned**: things they believe that peers might disagree with, and why.
6. **Industry news and trends**: new research, launches, regulation, viral debates. Always add their take; never just summarize.
7. **Other creators' posts**: a respectful response, extension or counterpoint (credit them).

**Angles** (turn raw material into a post):
- **Mistake**: "The mistake I made with X, and what I do now."
- **Myth**: "Everyone says X. Here's what I've seen instead."
- **How-to**: step-by-step for one specific outcome.
- **Framework**: a named, repeatable method (3-5 steps).
- **Checklist**: what to check before or after X.
- **Before/after**: what changed, with numbers.
- **Case study**: context → problem → what we did → result → lesson.
- **Teardown**: analyze a real example (a landing page, a pitch, a strategy) in their field.
- **Behind the scenes**: how something actually gets done.
- **Lesson from X**: what an experience outside work taught them about work (use sparingly).
- **Prediction**: what will change in their field and what to do about it.
- **Comparison**: X vs Y, when to use which.
- **Data/observation**: "I looked at 50 X. Here's the pattern."
- **Unpopular opinion (earned)**: backed by experience, not for shock.
- **Story → insight**: a specific moment, then the point.
- **Tools/stack**: what they use and why (real usage only).
- **Advice to my younger self / if I started over**: concrete, not generic.
- **Question post**: a genuinely open question to their audience, with their own answer first.
- **Milestone with substance**: an announcement that teaches something.
- **Series**: a recurring format ("Teardown Tuesday"). Recognizable series help people remember the author.

## 3. The quality bar for ideas

Before an idea goes into the backlog, it must pass:
1. **Only-you test:** does it contain the user's own story, data, observation or earned opinion? If a thousand people could post it unchanged, sharpen it or drop it.
2. **Audience test:** would someone in the target audience save it, send it to a colleague, or reply to it?
3. **Goal test:** does it move the user toward their goal (reach, trust or conversion)?
4. **Payoff test:** can you state the takeaway in one sentence?
5. **Truth test:** is the raw material real and supplied by the user? Ideas that need facts the user hasn't given are marked "needs: ...".

## 4. Hooks

The first line decides whether anyone reads the second. Large-dataset evidence (see `algorithm.md`): **story hooks** have the highest median engagement, then contrarian, statement, results and question hooks. **Short first lines** (under ~40 characters) beat long ones.

**Hook types, with examples:**
- **Story / moment**: "Tuesday, 11:40 pm. The database was gone."
- **Result**: "We cut onboarding from 14 days to 3."
- **Contrarian**: "Stop writing cover letters. Write this instead."
- **Confession**: "I lost a $40k client over one sentence."
- **Observation**: "I've read 300 data-analyst resumes this year."
- **Specific question** (not "Have you ever..."): "Why do 80% of SaaS pricing pages hide the price?"
- **Number + promise**: "7 SQL patterns I use every week:"
- **Stakes**: "Our runway was 9 weeks when I made this call."

**Rules:**
- Specific beats clever. Numbers, names and moments create curiosity on their own.
- The hook must be paid off early in the body. Bait-and-switch hooks hurt dwell time and trust.
- Line 2 earns the "see more" click: it raises the stakes or promises the payoff.
- Write 3-5 hook options of different types for every post, then pick.

## 5. Post structures

Pick the structure that fits the material. Don't force material into a template.

- **Story → lesson**: moment (hook) → what happened (with tension) → turning point → lesson → how the reader can apply it.
- **Contrarian**: the common belief → why it fails (from experience) → what to do instead → evidence → nuance (when the common belief *is* right).
- **How-to / list**: promise → numbered steps or items (3-7), each with a one-line why or example → the one thing that matters most.
- **Framework**: the problem → the named framework → each step with an example → when to use it.
- **Case study**: result (hook) → context → challenge → what we did (specific) → numbers → lesson.
- **Mistakes**: "N mistakes I see in X" → each mistake + the fix → the costliest one.
- **Observation / data**: what they looked at → the pattern → why it matters → what to do.
- **Teardown**: what we're looking at → what works → what doesn't → how to fix it.
- **Behind the scenes / build in public**: what we're working on → a real decision or number → what we learned → what's next.
- **Opinion on news**: the news (one line) → their take → why (experience) → implication for the audience.
- **Offer**: the problem → who it's for → what they get → proof → how to get it (one clear CTA).

## 6. Formats and specs

| Format | Use when | Specs and tips |
|---|---|---|
| **Text** | Stories, opinions, short how-tos; when the user writes well | Default 900-1,800 characters. Short paragraphs. |
| **Image + text** | The safest default; a real photo of the user, a screenshot, a chart, a simple diagram | Portrait 1080×1350 (4:5) takes the most feed space. Real photos usually beat stock images. Caption 800-1,000 characters. |
| **Document / carousel (PDF)** | Frameworks, step-by-step, checklists, teardowns, data | 1080×1350 portrait. 6-12 slides, one idea per slide, ≤ 30 words per slide, big type. Slide 1 = hook, last slide = summary or CTA. Caption 800-1,000 characters. Build with `SKILL_DIR/assets/carousel/`. |
| **Multi-image** | Event recaps, before/after, several screenshots | Strong on company pages. |
| **Video** | The user is good on camera, demos, talks | Personal-profile video reach is below average right now. If they do it: captions burned in, hook in the first 3 seconds, and a substantial text caption. Longer (3+ min) performs better than 30-60s. |
| **Poll** | Rarely: genuine research questions only | High impressions, low value. Poll bait is downranked. |
| **Newsletter** | Consultants, creators, executives with a sustainable cadence | Each edition notifies subscribers directly, bypassing the feed. A strong conversion asset. |
| **Article** | Evergreen deep-dives, search visibility | Low feed reach. |

## 7. Cadence by capacity

Match the user's real capacity from the brief. A sustainable cadence for 90 days beats an ambitious one for two weeks.

| Weekly time | Plan |
|---|---|
| ~30 min | Profile fixed. 10-15 min/day commenting, 3 days a week. 1 post every 1-2 weeks, or none. |
| 1-2 hrs | 1-2 posts/week + 10 min/day commenting. |
| 3-5 hrs | 3 posts/week (e.g. Tue/Thu/Sat) + 15-20 min/day commenting + replying to all comments. |
| 5+ hrs | 4-5 posts/week, a recurring series, a newsletter every 1-2 weeks. |

Space posts at least 24 hours apart. Batch-writing on one day a week is the most sustainable routine.

## 8. The engagement routine

Commenting on other people's posts is the most underrated growth lever, especially for accounts under ~5k followers. Comments put the user in front of other creators' audiences, and comment impressions now show in analytics.

**Daily 15-20 minutes:**
1. Reply to every genuine comment on their own recent posts (replies correlate with ~30% more engagement).
2. Leave 3-5 substantive comments on posts from the `people.md` list (peers, audience hubs, rising voices). Prefer posts less than a few hours old.
3. Accept or send 1-2 personalized connection requests to relevant people (a reason, not a pitch).

**What makes a good comment:** adds a specific example, a data point, a respectful counterpoint, or a sharp question; 2-5 sentences; sounds like a person; never pitches. Stay on-topic: commenting within their own field reinforces what they're known for.

**Never:** engagement pods, automated or AI-generated comments, "Great post!", mass tagging, or copy-paste DMs. LinkedIn suppresses all of these, and they damage reputation.

## 9. Repurposing

One substantial piece of raw material (a talk, a long article, a client project, a podcast appearance) can feed 5-10 posts:
- the core story as a story post;
- the framework as a carousel;
- each mistake or tip as its own post;
- the most surprising number as a data post;
- the strongest opinion as a contrarian post;
- a behind-the-scenes post about making it.

Space repurposed posts over weeks, and change the angle each time; don't paste the same text.

## 10. What to measure

Raw impressions are falling platform-wide (see `algorithm.md` §3), so judge posts against the user's **own median**, and weight the metrics that serve their goal:
- **Get hired:** profile views, search appearances, recruiter messages, interviews.
- **Clients / pipeline:** profile views from ICP titles, inbound DMs, calls booked, link clicks.
- **Audience:** followers gained per post, saves, sends, comments, newsletter subscribers.

Use `/linkedin:analyze` monthly with the analytics export to find the user's personal patterns.
