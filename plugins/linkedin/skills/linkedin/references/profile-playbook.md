# Profile playbook

Section-by-section guidance for auditing and writing LinkedIn profiles. Specs were checked against LinkedIn Help pages and reputable sources in October 2026. Confidence: **[H]** confirmed by LinkedIn, **[M]** consistent across reputable sources, **[L]** single source or inference. LinkedIn changes the product often, so if a user reports something different from what's here, believe them.

## 1. Specs and limits

| Field | Limit / spec | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | 220 characters. Only the first ~60-120 show in search cards, comments and invitations, so front-load. | [M] |
| About | 2,600 characters. Roughly the first 2-3 lines (~250-300 characters, depending on device) show before "see more". | [M] limit, [L] fold |
| Experience: title, company, location | 100 characters each | [M] |
| Experience: description | 2,000 characters per role | [M] |
| Post | 3,000 characters | [M] |
| Comment | 1,250 characters | [M] |
| Recommendation | 3,000 characters | [M] |
| Skills | Up to 100. Up to 5 "top skills" can be pinned in About. | [H] / [M] |
| Featured | No practical cap (400 shown). Not searchable, only visible to logged-in viewers. | [H] |
| Custom URL | 3-100 characters, letters/numbers (hyphens work), no "linkedin". Up to 5 changes per 6 months, and the old URL stays reserved for 6 months. | [H] |
| Profile photo | JPG/PNG, max 8 MB, minimum 400×400 px | [H] |
| Background banner | 1584×396 px recommended, JPG/PNG under 8 MB. On mobile the photo overlaps the left side, and the crop differs from desktop. | [H] |
| Name pronunciation | Record in the mobile app, up to 10 seconds | [H] |
| Open to Work | Visibility: all members (adds the #OpenToWork photo frame), recruiters only, or only you. Fields: titles, location types, locations, start date, employment types. | [H] |

**Things that no longer exist (don't recommend them):**
- Creator mode toggle (retired March 2024); members now choose Follow or Connect as their primary button.
- "Talks about" profile hashtags (removed February 2024).
- The gold "Community Top Voice" badge (retired October 2024). The blue Top Voice badge is invite-only.
- The free intro-card hyperlink. Free accounts link websites through Contact info.

**Premium-only or limited features:**
- The custom profile button (Visit website, Book an appointment...): select Premium Business, Sales Navigator and Recruiter Lite subscribers.
- "Write with AI" for headline and About: select Premium members.
- Services media, reviews and the "Request services" button need a premium tier, though the basic Services page is free.

## 2. How profiles get found

**LinkedIn people search** [H] is personalized to each searcher (their network, activity and history) and matches the query against profile content. LinkedIn publishes no field weights, and it warns that keyword stuffing can be flagged as spam. In 2025-2026 LinkedIn moved to **semantic search**: LLM-based retrieval over condensed "summarized profiles", plus natural-language search for Premium users (US, Nov 2025). Practical meaning: describe what the person does in **plain, specific language** that a reader would also use, not keyword lists. "I build fraud-detection models for fintech payments" beats "ML | AI | Data | Python | Fraud".

**LinkedIn Recruiter** [H] still runs Boolean keyword searches plus filters: job title, location, company, skills, school, industry, language. Keywords are matched across the headline, About, experience (titles and descriptions) and skills. Practical meaning for job seekers:
- Use the **standard market title** that recruiters type (current title field and headline).
- Have the target skills in the **Skills section** (structured filter) *and* in About/Experience text.
- Set **location** and Open to Work fields precisely; they are filters.
- Recruiter's "Active talent" spotlight includes members who recently updated their profile [H], so a refreshed profile is a small plus during a search.

**Profile completeness** [H]: LinkedIn's "All-Star" level needs photo, location, industry, education, current position, skills and a summary. Missing basics hurt search visibility and trust.

**The feed** [H/M]: LinkedIn Engineering (March 2026) describes the feed using members' profile information (headline, skills, work history, education) to understand them, with semantic rather than keyword matching. It's a reasonable inference, not something LinkedIn states outright, that **a profile whose topics match the member's posts helps those posts reach the right people**. Present it that way.

## 3. Headline

The most-seen line on the profile: it shows in search, under every post and comment, in invitations and in DMs.

**Principles**
- Lead with what the target reader is searching for or cares about: the target title, the result delivered, or the niche.
- The first ~70 characters must stand alone (truncated views).
- Specific beats impressive: "Helping B2B SaaS teams cut churn" > "Visionary Growth Leader".
- Use separators sparingly (`|` or `·`), 2-4 segments.
- No emoji strings, fancy unicode fonts, or a stack of buzzwords.
- Don't include "seeking opportunities" or "unemployed". Use Open to Work instead.

**Formulas** (adapt; don't fill them in mechanically)
- *Search-optimized (job seekers):* `{Target Title} | {Skill 1}, {Skill 2}, {Skill 3} | {Proof or domain}`
  → "Machine Learning Engineer | PyTorch, MLOps, Recommender Systems | Shipped models to 2M users"
- *Value proposition (consultants, founders, sales):* `I help {who} {achieve what} {how/with what}` or `{Result} for {who} | {Role} @ {Company}`
  → "Helping dental clinics cut no-shows by 30% | Co-founder & CEO @ Toothly"
- *Authority (experts, executives, creators):* `{Role/identity} | {Niche} | {Credential or proof}`
  → "CFO for seed-to-Series B SaaS | Took 3 companies through due diligence | Ex-Stripe"
- *Creator:* `{What you post about} for {who} | {proof / CTA}`
  → "Weekly teardowns of B2B landing pages | 40k readers of The Conversion Brief"
- *Career changer:* `{Target identity} | {Bridge from past} | {Proof in new field}`
  → "Product Designer | Former ER nurse designing tools clinicians actually use | 3 shipped health apps"

## 4. About

The landing page for the profile. Most visitors read only the part above "see more", so the first 2-3 lines must earn the click.

**Universal rules:** first person; short paragraphs; specific proof; plain language with the target keywords used naturally; a clear next step; no third-person bio; no "passionate results-driven professional"; never invent achievements.

**Structures by goal**
- *Job seeker:* (1) what they do and the role they're targeting, as a hook with a proof point · (2) 2-3 signature achievements with numbers · (3) how they work / what makes them different · (4) skills and tools in a readable line · (5) what they're looking for + how to reach them.
- *Founder:* (1) the problem their customers face, framed for the ICP · (2) what the company does about it and for whom · (3) proof: customers, results, traction they're happy to share · (4) why them (founder-market fit story, briefly) · (5) CTA: demo, DM, newsletter.
- *Consultant / freelancer:* (1) the client's problem in the client's words · (2) what they do and the result · (3) proof: results, named clients if allowed, a testimonial line · (4) how working together looks (offer, process) · (5) CTA with a booking link or "DM me 'X'".
- *Creator:* (1) what they post about and why someone should follow · (2) who it's for · (3) proof: audience, newsletter, notable work · (4) personal touch · (5) CTA: follow, subscribe.
- *Executive:* (1) mandate and mission · (2) track record with numbers · (3) leadership philosophy · (4) what the company is building, and hiring · (5) contact for press/speaking.
- *Career changer:* (1) the new identity and the bridge · (2) what transfers, with evidence · (3) what they've built in the new field · (4) what they're looking for.

**Length:** 1,200-2,000 characters fits most people. Use the full 2,600 only when every paragraph adds proof. A strong 600-character About beats a padded 2,600.

## 5. Experience

Recruiters and buyers skim the two most recent roles.

- **Title:** the standard market title (add the internal title in the description if it differs).
- **Description:** a 1-2 line scope statement (team size, budget, product, customers), then 3-5 outcome bullets.
- **Bullet formula:** *Accomplished X, measured by Y, by doing Z* (the "XYZ" pattern). → "Cut month-end close from 12 to 5 days by automating reconciliations in Python and dbt."
- No numbers available? Use scale, scope, before/after, or frequency ("for 40 enterprise clients", "weekly reporting to the CFO"). Ask the user for real numbers; never estimate them yourself.
- Add skills to each role, and media (decks, case studies, demos) where useful.
- Old or irrelevant roles: keep them short. Unexplained gaps: a one-line entry ("Career break: caregiving", which LinkedIn supports) beats silence.

## 6. Featured

Prime real estate near the top of the profile, showing up to ~3 cards before scrolling. Not indexed by search and only visible to logged-in members [H].

What to feature, by goal:
- *Job seeker:* portfolio, GitHub or case study, a talk, the best post demonstrating expertise.
- *Founder:* demo or product page, best customer story, newsletter or lead magnet.
- *Consultant:* booking link, case study with numbers, testimonial or Services.
- *Creator:* newsletter, best-performing posts, lead magnet or product.

Order matters: the first two cards get the most attention. Each card needs a clear title and a one-line description stating what the visitor gets.

## 7. Skills

- Up to 100 skills; pin up to 5 as **top skills**, chosen to match the target keywords [H/M].
- Skills are a structured filter in Recruiter, so the exact standard names matter ("Machine Learning", not "ML stuff").
- Remove outdated or irrelevant skills that dilute positioning (e.g. "Microsoft Word" for a senior engineer).
- Attach skills to experience entries so they show context.

## 8. Photo and banner

**Photo** (assess only from a screenshot): face takes ~60% of the frame, eyes visible, good light, a plain uncluttered background, current, and dressed the way they would be for their audience. Approachable expression. No group crops, sunglasses, logos or distant shots. Profiles with photos get far more views and connection acceptances; that's long-standing LinkedIn guidance.

**Banner** (1584×396): a positioning billboard. Options: (1) a one-line value proposition plus proof ("Helping SaaS teams cut churn · 120+ companies"), (2) a CTA ("Weekly newsletter: The Conversion Brief"), (3) a relevant photo (speaking, product, work). Keep the important text on the right two-thirds, since the photo overlaps the left on mobile. High contrast, ≤ 10 words, no tiny text. Free tools: Canva or Figma templates at 1584×396.

## 9. Recommendations and social proof

- 3+ recent (last ~2 years), specific recommendations from people relevant to the goal (managers for job seekers, clients for consultants).
- Make it easy: ask with a specific prompt, e.g.: "Would you be open to writing me a short LinkedIn recommendation? If it helps, the most useful thing would be a sentence on the {project} we did together and the impact it had on {outcome}."
- Certifications, publications, honors and Services reviews add trust when they're relevant to the goal.
- Verification (free identity or workplace verification) adds a trust badge [H].

## 10. Settings that matter

- **Custom URL:** `linkedin.com/in/firstname-lastname` or with a niche term if taken (`maya-chen-ml`).
- **Primary button: Follow vs Connect.** Follow suits people who post regularly and want an audience (creators, founders, consultants). Connect suits job seekers, students and people building a network. Change it in Settings → Visibility.
- **Open to Work:** recruiters-only if employed; public frame is a personal choice (it can attract recruiters but some hiring managers view it neutrally-to-negatively; there's no reliable data either way, so present it as a trade-off).
- **Services page:** for freelancers and consultants, even the free version helps them show up in service searches.
- **Contact info:** email or website, booking link.
- **Location and industry:** accurate (filters in search).
- **Name pronunciation and pronouns:** optional humanizing touches.

## 11. Other sections

Projects (great for students, career changers and engineers), Volunteer (relevant causes), Languages (with proficiency, important for international roles), Courses, Publications, Patents, Honors. Add a section only when it supports the goal; empty or irrelevant sections add noise.
